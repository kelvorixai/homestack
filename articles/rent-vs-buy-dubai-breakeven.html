Rent vs. buy in Dubai: the real breakeven math

"Buying is throwing away less money than renting" skips the part where buying has its own large upfront cost stack. The honest comparison is cumulative cost over time, not a monthly-payment-vs-monthly-rent snapshot.

The rent-vs-buy comparison usually gets reduced to "is the mortgage payment higher or lower than the rent." That's the wrong comparison — it ignores the large one-time costs on the buying side, and the fact that a mortgage payment includes principal you're building equity with, not pure cost the way rent is. The real question is cumulative cash outlay over a specific holding period.

The upfront cost stack on the buying side

Buying property in Dubai carries its own layered cost stack, separate from the purchase price: a down payment (commonly 20-25% of the property value for expat mortgage buyers, per UAE Central Bank mortgage cap regulations), a Dubai Land Department (DLD) transfer fee of 4% of the purchase price, a real estate agency commission typically around 2%, a mortgage registration fee of 0.25% of the loan amount, plus valuation and processing fees from the bank. Together, these commonly add up to roughly 7-9% of the property price on top of the down payment itself — due before you hold the keys.

Why the "mortgage payment vs. rent" comparison misleads

A portion of every mortgage payment is principal — money that builds ownership equity, not a cost that disappears the way rent does. Comparing the full mortgage payment to rent overstates buying's true monthly cost; the more honest comparison isolates interest, service charges, and maintenance (buying's actual "lost" costs) against rent (renting's full lost cost), then separately accounts for the equity being built.

Ongoing costs both sides carry

Renters pay rent and, typically, nothing else structural. Owners pay mortgage interest, DEWA and service charges (chargeable per square foot annually by the building/community, covering maintenance, security, and shared facilities), and are responsible for their own unit maintenance — costs a renter's landlord absorbs instead.

What actually determines the breakeven point

FactorEffect on breakeven
Longer holding periodFavors buying — upfront costs amortize over more years
Higher annual rent growthFavors buying — renting's cumulative cost rises faster
Higher mortgage interest rateFavors renting — more of each payment is "lost" to interest
High service charges on the propertyFavors renting — adds an ongoing cost renters don't carry
Property value appreciation (or decline)Independent variable — can swing the outcome either way, and is the least predictable input

Commonly cited breakeven estimates for Dubai put the crossover somewhere in the 4-7 year holding-period range under typical assumptions — but that range moves substantially with the specific mortgage rate, service charge level, and rent growth assumed, which is why a generic rule of thumb is far less reliable than running the actual numbers for a specific property and mortgage offer.

The actual question worth running

Not "is buying better than renting" as a universal answer, but "given my actual down payment, the mortgage rate I'm quoted, this specific property's service charges, and how long I actually plan to stay — where's my breakeven, and am I past or short of that holding period." Plugging real numbers from a real mortgage offer and a real rental comparable into the framework above gives an answer specific to the decision at hand, rather than a generic market-wide rule.
