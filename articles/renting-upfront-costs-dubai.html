The Numbers

Renting in Dubai: the true upfront cost beyond the annual rent

The number in the listing is the annual rent. What you actually need in the bank before you get keys is that figure plus five or six additional lines — some refundable, some not — that regularly add another 8-15% on top.

Dubai rental listings quote one number — the annual rent, often payable in one to four cheques. That number is the anchor, but it is not the amount you need available to actually move in. Layered on top are a refundable security deposit, a non-refundable agency commission, a government registration fee, a utility deposit, and — easy to forget until the day arrives — the cost of physically moving your belongings in.

Security deposit: refundable, but due upfront

Landlords typically hold a security deposit of 5% of the annual rent for an unfurnished unit, or 10% for a furnished one, paid as a separate cheque at signing. It's refundable at the end of the tenancy, minus any deductions for damage beyond normal wear — but it's still cash you need available on day one, on top of the rent itself.

Real estate agency commission: the standard is 5% plus VAT

The market-standard agency commission in Dubai is 5% of the annual rent, plus 5% VAT on that commission — so effectively 5.25% of the annual rent, non-refundable, paid once at signing. This applies whether you found the unit through a portal or an agent walked you through it; it's the standard fee structure across the market, not something unique to one agency.

Ejari registration: mandatory, and cheap relative to the rest

Every Dubai tenancy contract must be registered with Ejari, the Dubai Land Department's official tenancy registration system — required for utility connections, visa applications tied to the address, and as legal proof of tenancy. Registration typically costs around AED 220, whether done directly or through a typing/registration center, plus a small service fee if a third party handles it for you.

DEWA connection and security deposit

Connecting electricity and water through DEWA (Dubai Electricity and Water Authority) requires a refundable security deposit — commonly around AED 2,000 for an apartment or AED 4,000 for a villa — plus a smaller non-refundable activation fee. Like the tenancy deposit, this comes back when you disconnect and move out (minus any unpaid bills), but it's still money you need on hand at move-in.

Moving costs: the line people forget to budget

Local moving costs vary widely by volume and distance, but a studio-to-one-bedroom move within Dubai commonly runs somewhere in the AED 500–1,500 range with a local moving company, more for larger units or if items need to be sourced (furniture, appliances) rather than just transported.

Added together, the realistic upfront cash needed to move into a Dubai rental commonly runs 10-16% above the quoted annual rent — before furnishing an unfurnished unit, which is its own separate budget entirely. On an AED 80,000/year apartment, that's roughly an extra AED 8,000-13,000 that the listing price alone doesn't tell you about.

The actual question before signing

Not "can I afford the annual rent" but "do I have the annual rent plus the full move-in stack available at once, in cash, before the first cheque clears." Multi-cheque payment plans (splitting the annual rent into 2-4 cheques) ease the ongoing burden, but the deposit, agency fee, Ejari, and DEWA charges are typically due at signing regardless of how the rent itself is split.
